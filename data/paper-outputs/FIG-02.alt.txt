Three panels. Panels A (chains) and B (pieces) are dot plots of the whole-answer accuracy of the Haiku-medium vote of 40 samples. Puzzles are grouped by their single-sample accuracy bin (bins of 10 points, each labelled by its range on the x axis, in two staggered rows) and, within each bin, by thirds of how often their wrong samples agree with each other: up to three dots per bin, from left to right the lowest, middle and highest third (three shades of Haiku’s blue, darkest for the lowest third), each with a 95% bar. No line joins the dots: each cell holds 1 to 6 puzzles, so the dots show where a cell sits, not a trend. One more dot, in grey and labelled “too few wrong samples”, in a column of its own right of the 90–100 bin (whose puzzles they are), stands for the puzzles with fewer than 2 wrong samples, too few to split into thirds: 6 chains and 5 pieces. The number of puzzles in each cell prints in one row under the dots, in light grey bands that run behind every second bin and behind the grey dot’s column. No value is printed beside a dot; the cells that are neither at zero nor at the top of the axis are, in the chains panel: in the 10–20 bin the lowest third 83.3%, and in the 40–50 bin the middle third 66.7% and the highest third 16.7%. In the pieces panel: in the 20–30 bin the lowest third 66.7%, and in the 40–50 bin the highest third 37.5%. In the chains panel the lowest, middle and highest thirds reach 83.3%, 0.0% and 0.0% in the 10–20 bin, 100.0%, 0.0% and 0.0% in the 30–40 bin, and 100.0%, 66.7% and 16.7% in the 40–50 bin. Panel C shows, as horizontal bars, that over both shapes a pair of wrong Haiku-medium samples gave the same wrong answer in 33.4% of pairs and the same first slip in 50.3% of the pairs whose first slips can both be placed; on chains alone the shares are 27.9% and 48.9%, on pieces alone 40.4% and 51.9%. Where a cell’s vote is right on all or none of its puzzles (a dot at zero or at the top of the axis), its bootstrap interval has no width and its bar shows the exact 95% interval over that cell’s puzzles from free analysis 4 (post hoc).
